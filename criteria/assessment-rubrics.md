# Fixed assessment criteria — Medical-Chat
Use these rubrics in every trial lesson (Section D) and when updating programmes.

Estimate a **working CEFR band** for clinical/medical communication (not academic essay writing).
Prefer the lower band if performance is mixed.

---

## 1. Overall working level (quick map)

| Band | What you hear in a medical context |
|------|-------------------------------------|
| **A2** | Short phrases; heavy L1 influence; needs constant support; cannot run a history alone |
| **B1** | Can do a simple history with support; frequent grammar/vocab gaps; pace uneven; meaning usually clear |
| **B2** | Independent history/explanation with some hesitation; errors do not block care communication; can self-correct sometimes |
| **C1** | Fluent clinical interaction; precise vocab; controls register; rare meaning-blocking errors |
| **C2** | Effortless, nuanced, near-native professional control |

Self-assessed “I’m fluent” on Preply is **not** the final level. Confirm in activities.

---

## 2. Skill rubrics (score each: Emerging / Developing / Secure)

### A. Grammar accuracy
- **Emerging:** Frequent basic errors (tenses, articles, word order) that slow or confuse the listener
- **Developing:** Errors present but meaning usually survives; inconsistent control of past/present and questions
- **Secure:** Stable control of core structures; remaining errors are advanced or rare

**Trial focus samples:** question formation, past simple vs present perfect for history, conditionals for advice (if relevant).

### B. Medical / clinical English
- **Emerging:** Lacks core symptom and examination language; translates literally from L1
- **Developing:** Knows common terms but order of history is weak; limited empathy/explanation phrases
- **Secure:** Uses appropriate clinical vocabulary and structure (e.g. open → focused questions, summary, next steps)

### C. Pronunciation / intelligibility
- **Emerging:** Listener must ask for repetition often; sound contrasts or word stress block understanding
- **Developing:** Understandable with some effort; a few high-impact sounds/stress patterns need work
- **Secure:** Clear at normal clinical speed; accent may remain but does not block care

**Arabic L1 watch-list (use when relevant):** /p–b/, /v–f/, /θ/, final consonants, vowel length, word stress on medical terms (e.g. *abdomen*, *respiratory*, *investigation*).

### D. Fluency & pace
- **Emerging:** Very slow or rushed; long pauses; cannot sustain a 60–90s turn
- **Developing:** Can sustain short turns; pacing uneven under pressure (e.g. role-play)
- **Secure:** Steady, examiner-appropriate pace; can slow down on purpose when explaining

### E. Confidence & interaction
- **Emerging:** Avoids elaboration; gives one-word answers; visibly blocked
- **Developing:** Willing but tense; needs prompts to expand or check understanding
- **Secure:** Initiates clarification, summarises, checks understanding; professional presence

---

## 3. How to phrase the result to the student (calm, precise)

Template:
> “From today’s tasks, your **working level for medical communication** is around **[B1/B2…]**.  
> Strengths: [1–2 concrete points].  
> Priority areas: [1–3 areas].  
> We’ll build a plan that targets those first so every session moves you toward [OSCE / OET / hospital communication].”

Never shame self-assessment. Reframe:
> “Self-ratings are useful; today’s activities show us the most efficient starting point.”

---

## 4. Priority ranking rule
Order work by **impact on the student’s stated goal**:
1. Blocks exam/station success or patient safety communication  
2. High-frequency in every consultation  
3. Polish / advanced accuracy  

Example OSCE student: intelligibility + history structure before advanced hedging.
